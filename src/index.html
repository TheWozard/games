Logic

  • SudokuClassic or Killer: fill the grid so every row, column and box has 1–9.

    Sudoku

    Fill the grid so every row, column and 3×3 box holds 1–9 once. Pick Classic, Killer or Jigsaw under +.

    Killer: only a few digits are given. Each colored cage’s digits add up to the number on its top edge, with no digit repeated inside a cage, and a full cage with the wrong total turns red.

    Jigsaw: the 3×3 boxes become nine outlined shapes of nine cells, and each shape holds 1–9 once.

    Pick a tool under the board (the eraser, the notebook for notes or the pencil to set a number) and a number, then tap cells. The pencil places the number in a cell, replacing what’s there, and clears it from the notes around it; tap a cell holding that number to clear it. The notebook adds the number as a small note; drag across cells to mark several, or start on one that has it to take it away. The eraser clears a cell; drag to clear several. Repeated digits turn red.

  • ShadingNonogram, Star Battle, Nurikabe, Hitori and Light Up: decide which cells to fill.

    Shading

    Five puzzles, picked under +. Each has one solution.

    Nonogram: the numbers beside each row and above each column are the lengths of its runs of shaded cells, in order, with at least one empty cell between runs. A clue dims when its line matches. Two optional rules: Colors, where each number is in its run’s color, and runs of different colors may touch; and Mystery, where some numbers are hidden as ?. On a narrow screen a big grid slides sideways: drag on the numbers along the top.

    Star Battle: place stars so every row, column and outlined region holds the same number (one, or two on Hard). Stars never touch, not even at a corner.

    Nurikabe: shade cells to make one connected sea. Each number is an island of exactly that many unshaded cells, with only that number in it. Islands don’t touch side by side, and the sea has no 2×2 block.

    Hitori: shade cells so no number repeats in a row or column. Shaded cells never touch side by side, and the unshaded ones stay connected.

    Light Up: place lamps. Each lights its row and column up to a wall. Every white cell must be lit, no lamp may light another, and a number on a wall is how many lamps sit beside it.

    Tap a cell to place what the circle at the bottom right shows (a shade or color, a star or lamp, or a note: an X, a dot or a circle), and again to clear it. Tap the circle to switch. Drag along a row or column to do the same to every cell you pass. Marks that break a rule turn red.

  • MinesClear the field using the counts of hidden mines next to each cell. No guessing needed.

    Mines

    Reveal every cell that isn’t a mine. Each number counts the mines in the eight cells around it.

    Tap to reveal. Long-press (or right-click) to flag a mine.

    Tap a number whose mines are all flagged to clear the cells around it.

    Your first tap is always safe, and every board can be cleared without guessing.

Paths

  • LoopDraw one closed loop along the dots; each number says how many of its sides the loop uses.

    Loop

    Draw a single closed loop along the lines between dots. It can’t branch or cross itself.

    A number in a cell says how many of that cell’s four sides the loop uses. Cells without a number can have any.

    Tap a side to draw a line, tap it again to mark it ✕ (not part of the loop), and once more to clear it. Numbers dim when they’re met and turn red when there are too many.

  • BridgesLink the islands with bridges so each has its number, without crossings, all connected.

    Bridges

    Each island’s number is how many bridges it needs.

    Bridges run straight across or down, at most two between the same islands. They can’t cross, and every island must end up connected.

    Drag from one island to another to build a bridge; drag again for a double. Swipe across a bridge to remove one, or tap the gap to cycle 0 → 1 → 2.

  • FlowJoin matching dots with paths that never cross and fill every square.

    Flow

    Join each pair of matching dots with a path through neighboring squares. Paths can’t cross or share a square, and the puzzle is done when every square is filled.

    Drag from a dot to draw. Drag back over a path to shorten it, or start on a path to pick it up from there. Other colors block the way: move them first.

  • UntangleDrag the dots until none of the lines cross. No clock, no rush.

    Untangle

    Drag the dots until no two lines cross.

    A dot resting on a line it isn’t part of counts as a crossing too (it gets a red ring), and a dot dropped on another is nudged clear. Crossing lines show in red.

    For a new puzzle, pick how many dots, a style (Lines, a dense Mesh, or sparse Loops) and how the dots start: round a Circle, along a Spiral, in three Rows, on a Grid, or Scattered. The whole screen is yours to spread them out. Every puzzle can be untangled.

Arrange

  • Sliding BlocksSlide the blocks aside to clear a way out for the red one.

    Sliding Blocks

    Get the red block out: clear its row and drag it off past the arrow on the right.

    Drag a block to slide it. Blocks only move along their length, and can’t pass through each other.

    New Game (+) has four kinds: Classic; Pillars, with a few fixed squares that never move; Big, on an 8×8 board; and Two Keys, where a blue block leaves through the bottom too, and both must get out. A key block that reaches its exit is gone, and leaves its squares free.

    The number at the top is the fewest moves it can be done in. Sliding a block any distance is one move. Each kind has 100 levels, from Easy through Medium to Hard, played in order, a little harder each time; New Game (+) lets you pick any one.

  • Color SortPour colored layers between tubes until every tube holds one color.

    Color Sort

    Sort the colors so each tube ends up full of a single color (or empty).

    Tap a tube to pick it up, then tap another to pour into it. The run of matching color on top pours across, as much as fits, but only into an empty tube or onto the same color.

    Every deal can be sorted, but some pours lead to a dead end. If you run out of moves, Undo to try another way, or start a new deal with +.

  • GradientSwap tiles until the colors flow smoothly from corner to corner.

    Gradient

    Swap tiles until the colors flow smoothly from corner to corner.

    Tap two tiles to swap them, or drag one onto another. Tiles with a dot are fixed.

    The marks beside each row and under each column light up when that line is in place.

Cards & tiles

  • SolitaireKlondike or FreeCell: build each suit up from Ace to King. Every deal can be won.

    Solitaire

    Two games, picked under +. In both, move every card to the four foundations along the top, building each suit up from Ace to King, and build the columns down in alternating colors (a red 7 on a black 8). Tap a card to send it to its best spot. Wide columns pan sideways under your finger.

    Klondike: seven columns, most cards face down. Only a King can go into an empty column, and moving a column’s last face-up card turns over the one beneath it. Tap the stock (bottom right) to draw one card or three; when it runs out, tap again to turn the pile over. Once every card is showing, the rest goes home by itself.

    FreeCell: eight columns, every card face up. Any card can go into an empty column, and each free cell along the bottom holds one card. You can move a run of cards at once if there’s room to move it one card at a time through the free cells and empty columns. Drag a card to put it exactly where you want. Fewer free cells make it harder.

  • MahjongClear the stacked tiles by matching free pairs. Every deal can be cleared.

    Mahjong

    Clear the board by matching pairs of identical tiles. Flowers and seasons are the exception: any flower matches any other flower, and any season any other season.

    A tile is free when nothing lies on it and its left or right side is open; dimmed tiles aren’t free yet. Tap a free tile, then its match.

    If no matching pairs are free, a Shuffle button appears to mix up the tiles that are left.

    In New Game, pick one layout or several: each deal takes one of those you picked at random.

    Easy deals forgive a careless match. Harder ones hide traps: take the wrong pair early and its twin can end up stuck, so look ahead before you match.
